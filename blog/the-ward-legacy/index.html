The Ward legacy: passing on what works

November 10, 2026 · Chris Ward, Ogden, Utah

Grow With AI is a new name on an old habit. In 1971 David Ward started Z-Clear by showing strangers how his lens cleaner worked, and his kids came along to help. Three generations later, the family still believes the best way to share something useful is to teach it in person, step by step.

A recipe, a grocery store and a family that showed up

David Ward came up with his lens cleaner recipe in Malad, Idaho. He did not have a sales team. He had a table at the local grocery store and the patience to show each person what it did.

His kids helped on a lot of those days. They learned the product by watching people try it, and they learned that a good demonstration does more than a good pitch.

His grandson, Chris Ward, owns Z-Clear today and runs it from Ogden. It is still made by hand in small batches, and it is still sold face to face at many events every year. The full story is on the Z-Clear blog.

What three generations taught us about helpers

  1. Prove it on your own business first. Every helper we offer runs in Chris's own companies before anyone else gets it.
  2. Keep a person in charge. Helpers write drafts. Anything that spends money or can't be undone waits for your yes, and silence is never a yes.
  3. Be honest about what it is. Every helper says it's AI, never asks for your passwords, and stops when you type PAUSE.
  4. Leave the personal part to people. The customer who has bought from your family for years should still hear from you.
